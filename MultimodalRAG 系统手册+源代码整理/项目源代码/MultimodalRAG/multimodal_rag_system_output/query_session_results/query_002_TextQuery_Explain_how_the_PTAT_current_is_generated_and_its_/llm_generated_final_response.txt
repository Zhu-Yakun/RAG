The PTAT (Proportional To Absolute Temperature) current in a bandgap circuit is generated through the following process:

1. **Presence of PTAT and CTAT Components:** In a bandgap reference circuit, the PTAT voltage is typically provided by the voltage drop across a diode or a transistor's base-emitter junction (such as \( \Delta V_{BE} \) from Q1, Q2, and R0-R2 in the Bandgap68 circuit). This voltage varies linearly with temperature.

2. **Temperature Variation:** As the temperature changes, the voltage drop across these components also changes proportionally. This voltage change is then used to generate a current that varies with temperature.

The role of the PTAT current in a bandgap circuit is crucial for achieving temperature stability:

- **Temperature Compensation:** The PTAT current helps in compensating for the temperature variations in other parts of the circuit. By generating a current that varies with temperature, it can be used to counterbalance the effects of temperature on other circuit components.

- **Stabilizing the Reference Voltage:** In a bandgap reference circuit, the PTAT current is often used in conjunction with a CTAT (Coefficient Temperature-Dependent) current to create a stable reference voltage. The PTAT current's contribution ensures that the reference voltage remains relatively constant over a range of temperatures.

In summary, the PTAT current in a bandgap circuit is generated through temperature-sensitive components and plays a critical role in temperature compensation and stabilizing the reference voltage.
